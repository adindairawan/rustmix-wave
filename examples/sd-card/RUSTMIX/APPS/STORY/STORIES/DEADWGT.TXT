Chapter 1: Code Black at St. Jude's

Four hours and twelve minutes.

That is how long it has been since the seven-year-old girl in Exam Room 4 sank her teeth into your left forearm.

You stare into the cracked mirror above the scrub sink in Sub-Level 2's locker room, gripping the porcelain edge until your knuckles turn white. Your name badge—*Dr. Alex Vance, Chief Night-Shift Trauma Resident*—hangs crookedly from your blood-spattered scrubs. For six years, this basement and the ER pit above it have been your entire world. You grew up on scholarship scraps, worked forty-eight-hour shifts on black coffee, and earned a reputation as the only doctor in St. Jude's whose hands never shake during a code.

They are shaking now.

At 9:40 PM tonight, paramedics brought in little Maya Lin from a quarantined commuter train—Patient Zero of whatever nightmare has swallowed the city. While you were intubating her, her eyes milked over into necrotic grey and she bit clean through your latex glove. Everyone else bitten tonight died of violent hemorrhagic fever within thirty minutes and stood back up three minutes later—slow, unthinking, and driven only to feed.

You locked yourself in this basement bathroom at eleven o'clock with a lethal syringe of morphine, refusing to turn into a monster. At midnight, black veins spider-webbed up your arm and your heart stopped on the cold tile floor.

Then you gasped, woke up in the dark, and watched the black veins recede into a raised crescent of silvery-grey scar tissue that burns like dry ice against your pulse. Your temperature is 99.1. Your mind is razor-sharp. You are the only person in this hospital whose blood fought the infection off.

And you can't tell a living soul. Because upstairs, the military Bio-Containment Taskforce has declared St. Jude's a total biological loss—and under Code Black protocol, anyone with a bite mark is executed on sight.

A heavy, triple-beat knock rattles the steel locker room door.

Two short raps, a pause, one long.

Your breath catches in your throat. Only one person on earth knocks like that.

Dr. Julian Mercer. St. Jude's youngest attending Pediatric Surgeon. For four years, Julian was your best friend, your fiercest rival, and the man you planned to marry. You shared a cramped fourth-floor walk-up and a chipped French press—until six months ago in October, when Julian worked an illegal thirty-six-hour shift at the hospital's underfunded free clinic. Exhausted to the point of blindness, his scalpel slipped during emergency surgery on a city councilman's nephew. The boy died on the table.

The corrupt hospital board prepared to send Julian to prison for ten years to cover up their own staffing cuts. Julian didn't ask you to save him. You did it behind his back—forging a federal safety clearance and altering the digital anesthesia logs to blame a "tainted pharmaceutical shipment." When Julian discovered you had committed a felony to save him, the guilt broke him. He walked out in the pouring rain the next morning to keep you from going down with him. You haven't spoken a personal word in six months.

"Alex?" Julian's voice filters through the brushed steel, raw with smoke and panic. "I know you're in there! The military strike team is wiring the basement foundation pillars with C4—they're collapsing the hospital in ten minutes to trap the four thousand infected inside! Open the door, please!"
